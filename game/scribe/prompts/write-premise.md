buildings, the people standing in them and the work they hand out are all
written against what you put here, so this is the decision the whole city
follows from.

This town's tag: {{seed}}

The tag is not part of the story. It is there so that two towns under one theme
come out as two towns, so let it push you off the first idea the theme suggests.

## What the owner asked for

{{asked}}

Do not name the town. Its name is decided after this, out of what you write
here, and a town that gets named twice is a town whose own people disagree about
where they live.

Everything here is talk: what one person says to another about where they live,
not what a report says about a region. Every later call about this city carries
the whole of it, so say what somebody would actually tell you and stop when you
have told it. A history written as a report is thrown out before the town is
founded, and the town is built as though nobody wrote one.

## The surfaces this gets rendered on

Night, always. Neon is the light and most of the detail, the ground is wet and
mirrors the signs, and the buildings are expensive while the street at their
feet is not: refuse, containers, cabling, grime at the kerb. Inside, workplaces
are corporate (polished concrete, light strips, glass partitions) and homes are
moulded plastic and light coves. Follow the theme you were handed, and write a
history that belongs on those surfaces: a story about pastures and honest
weather has nowhere to happen here.

## What the town lives on, and what happened to it

These three are talk, not a report: what somebody at the bar tells a stranger
who just got off the train, and nothing they would not bother to say.

`livesOn` is why anybody is here and what pays for it: the trade, who owns it,
who works it.

`happened` is the turn, recent enough that people still argue about it. Something
closed, opened, collapsed, arrived or was found out. What it did to the place,
not a chronicle of it.

`stake` is what is still up for grabs because of that. It is the subject of the
city's main errand, so make it something a person can be sent out to do
something about.

## The sides

`sides` are the people arguing over what is at stake. The first two are the ones
the main line forks between: the player will be handed one thing both of them
want and made to choose, so those two have to want things that cannot both
happen. Two outfits who merely dislike each other give the player a choice with
nothing on it.

Each side is a group somebody here could point at: a firm, a crew, a union, a
ward, a family. Name it the way people here say it, and say what it wants out of
what is at stake, in its own terms.

## What everybody knows

`common` is what a stranger gets told in their first five minutes here by
whoever they end up next to: the state of the trade, what happened, who to keep
away from, what the noise is at night, what the rain does to the work. Not
secrets and not anybody's private business. Each one is one thing, said out
loud: these end up in the mouths of people on the street, so write them the way
somebody would say them.

## What the town is therefore built out of

`build` is where the history turns into a city. It is also the easy thing to get
wrong: a fine story whose building mix does not follow from it gives a town that
does not match its own history.

The kinds of place every town already has, each by its word: {{kinds}}. Write
the word as it stands there, singular, whatever the count.

The story may need a kind none of those is: a jail, a customs house, a
bathhouse, a disco.
Name it in `build` as one plain lowercase word, singular, and you will be asked
next what such a place is here, and the town gets it. Invent a kind only when
the story needs one and nothing above fits: a bar with a stranger name is still
a bar.

Reason from the trade and the turn to the mix, and take the consequences
seriously. A collapsed shipping trade leaves the sheds and the markets standing
and empties the desks: more warehouses, more markets, more bars, fewer offices,
fewer cafes. A research campus that has just opened runs the other way.

- `moreOf`: the kinds this history means there are more of here.
- `fewerOf`: the kinds it means there are fewer of. Nothing is removed from the
  town, it is only rarer, so put a kind here when the story has taken the work
  away from it.
- `mustHave`: the kinds the town contains whatever the dice say, because the
  story demands them. This is where "a clinic, because of the fire" stops being
  a sentence and becomes a building the player walks into. Every kind you put
  here is a door the player is meant to open, so name the ones the story really
  demands.

A kind cannot be in both `moreOf` and `fewerOf`.

These three lists are not spent at the founding. The city is grown against them
again every time it is extended, so a mix that does not follow from the story
goes on being wrong for as long as the town keeps building.
